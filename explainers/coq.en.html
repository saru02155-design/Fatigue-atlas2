FATIGUE EVIDENCE ATLAS · Intervention explainer · pilot

Coenzyme Q10

Supplementation with a cofactor of mitochondrial electron transport. Formulations, doses and patient populations differ across trials.

How to read the findings

A 121-person Long COVID crossover trial did not establish superiority on its principal outcomes; a smaller fibromyalgia trial reported improvements.

Proposed mechanism

Its established biochemical function does not imply that supplementation reliably improves chronic fatigue.

Specific trial conditions and findings

Selected records from the Atlas; these are not all independent RCTs and have not undergone a new full-text audit.

Long COVID: crossover RCT, n=121

Population
Long COVID
Intervention / comparator
CoQ10 500 mg/day for six weeks, then a four-week washout before crossover.
Reported outcome
Composite symptom difference: −1.18 (95% CI −3.54 to 1.17; p=0.32). No superiority on the principal outcomes.

Fibromyalgia: placebo-controlled study, n=20

Population
fibromyalgia
Intervention / comparator
CoQ10 300 mg/day for 40 days.
Reported outcome
Reported improvement in FIQ and fatigue-related subitems; small and short-duration trial.
Open source ↗
Mechanistic background and limitations

Its established biochemical function does not imply that supplementation reliably improves chronic fatigue.

Evidence for a biological action is distinct from evidence that it causes improvement in fatigue or PEM.
